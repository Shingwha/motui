# motui 1.2.3 — a band that knows its region

**A chrome band now says what it decorates. A scrollbar is the pane's
window, not its edge — so a pinned header, footer or composer keeps its
column, and the thumb describes the rectangle it actually scrolls.**

## The complaint

A pane's document may pin rows above and below the scrolling body: a
toolbar, a status line, a chat composer. A pane-level `Scrollbar` on such a
pane painted its track and thumb over those rows too, and a left press on
their column paged the pane. Two rectangles were in play and only one of
them was written down: the bar's *thumb* was already computed from the
window (`ScrollState(viewport_height=len(body_rows))`) while its *extent*
was the pane's whole height. The thumb was stretched, the drag mapping was
measured against the wrong track, and the pinned rows were part of the
gesture.

## The model

The kernel already had both rectangles and their vocabulary — `Pane.rect`
and the layout's `viewport_start` / `body_rows` — and it already said the
rule in `_plan_chrome`'s docstring: *a left/right band shares its rows with
the viewport*. The band just measured itself across the pane anyway. So the
fix is a word, not a special case:

```python
Chrome(edge, size, paint, name="", scope="pane")   # "pane" | "body"
```

`"pane"` (the default) spans the pane's whole rectangle — the split's
separator, a border. `"body"` spans the pane's scrolling window: the rows
between its fixed regions, which is what a scrollbar decorates. That is the
split every comparable toolkit makes — a divider belongs to the layout, a
scrollbar to the scroll container — and it costs no new data: the extent is
the grant clipped to the window the layout already publishes.

A body band is a `"left"`/`"right"` band only: a top/bottom band *owns*
rows instead of sharing them, so a mismatched pair raises `ValueError`
rather than being silently ignored. `Pane.band(chrome)` — the one
derivation the compositor's paint and a host's press test share — answers
the window's rectangle for a body band, `None` before the pane has been
composed and `None` for a pane whose fixed regions fill it. `Chrome.paint`
receives the band's own length, so a painter draws every cell it owns and
none it does not: no padding, no "painting less than granted" convention.

## What changed for a bar

`Scrollbar` registers its chrome body-scoped. Nothing else in it moved —
its strip, thumb length, travel, drag arithmetic, track paging, ticker and
cache all read the same numbers, because the numbers are now the window's:

- the strip covers the window's rows and nothing else;
- a press on a pinned row's column is not the bar's;
- the thumb's length and travel, and the document rows a drag maps per
  strip row, describe the window the scroll state belongs to;
- a pane with no window (fixed regions fill it) paints no strip, routes no
  press, and does not error.

Panes with no fixed regions render byte-identically — the golden topics and
the framebench `render-hash` are unchanged, and the demo was not touched.

## Migration

Nothing to do for a pane without fixed regions. A host that wants the old
full-edge presentation registers its own band —
`Chrome("right", size, paint)` — because `"pane"` is the default scope. The
budget is unchanged either way: a band's cells come off the pane's edge, so
the rows a body band does not span keep that column empty. Giving them a
full width would need a body width and a fixed width in `Viewport.layout`,
and a `ScreenLayout` whose rows are not uniform — `extract`, find, the
selection overlay and the frame's row memo all assume uniformity. That trade
is recorded in `docs/DESIGN.md` as a known limitation rather than paid for
with one column of chrome; the paragraph is the door if a body-only gutter
ever earns it.
